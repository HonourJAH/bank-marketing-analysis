# Findings

Each section answers one business question from the clean data (41,176 records). Rows are records, not unique customers, because the data has no customer ID. Differences in yes rate show associations, not causes. The overall yes rate of 11.27% is the baseline for every comparison.

## Q1. Overall result and calling effort

**Answer**
- 4,639 of 41,176 records subscribed to a term deposit: 11.27%, about 1 in 9.
- Subscribers took on average 2.05 contacts in this campaign and non-subscribers 2.63. The median is 2 for both groups.
- Non-subscribers are 88.73% of records but account for 91.0% of contacts (96,216 of 105,735).
- Across the campaign, 22.8 contacts were made for every subscription.

**What it means for the business**
Most records took about two contacts whatever the result, and calling effort is only slightly tilted toward customers who did not subscribe. The higher average among non-subscribers, with equal medians, points to a minority of heavily contacted customers who rarely subscribe. Question 6 looks at where extra calling stops paying off.

**Limits**
- The 22.8 contacts per subscription is a campaign-wide ratio that includes contacts made to customers who never subscribed. It is not the cost of winning one customer; the data has no call costs or deposit values.
- Subscribers may stop being called once they say yes, so comparing contact counts between the two groups does not show whether more calls reduce the chance of a yes.
- Contacts counted are only those in the current campaign.

## Q2. Age and life stage

**Answer**
- The yes rate is U-shaped across age. The overall baseline is 11.27%.
- Customers aged 65 and over subscribed at 47.28% (313 of 662) and those under 25 at 23.99% (256 of 1,067). Ages 35-44 and 45-54 were below the baseline, at 8.66% and 8.64%.
- The high rate at the old end holds in every five-year step from 65: 47.42%, 40.70%, 52.94% and 51.33% (119 to 199 records each).
- At the young end the signal comes from ages 20-24 (22.38%, 222 of 992). Ages 15-19 have only 75 records and are not interpreted.
- The rate jumps at 60: 10.70% for ages 55-59 (325 of 3,036) against 30.00% for ages 60-64 (159 of 530). The number of records also falls sharply at 60.
- Under 25 and 65 and over together are 4.20% of records but 12.27% of subscribers. Ages 35-54 are 53.90% of records and 41.39% of subscribers.

**What it means for the business**
Customers aged 60 and over, and aged 20-24, respond far better than average, but they are a small part of the call list. Most of the list, ages 35-54, responds below average. Whether shifting effort towards the responsive ages would pay off is a question these data cannot answer on their own.

**Limits**
- The data shows an association with age, not why. The jump at 60 is not explained, and retirement is only a guess until age is compared with job (Q3).
- Age overlaps with job and with the time of the call, so part of any age pattern may come from those (Q3, Q9, Q10).
- The age bands are my choice. The broad 55-64 band hid the jump at 60, so band edges can change what a result seems to say.
- Rows are records, not unique customers.
